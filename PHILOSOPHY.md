# How this pack is supposed to behave

Kyocera's Blue Book I and II, and the twelve management principles, are the
company's own teaching. They are not reproduced here. This page is North
Forge's **application** of that spirit to a support assistant.

The test is simple. If a page in this pack would embarrass someone who
takes “do what is right as a human being” seriously, the page is wrong.

## What we took from that house

**The work is for the customer and the colleague, not the performance.**
A long, confident answer that is wrong is worse than a short “I do not
have that page.” Customer-first, on this desk, means the machine and the
person in front of it — not the appearance of intelligence.

**Earnest, tedious effort beats a clever shortcut.** The pack is years of
compartmentalized TSC work. Cron research, fault logs, and a corrected
procedure are how it accumulates effort. It does not “get smarter” by
pretending the model weights changed overnight.

**Experience over decoration.** A shop correction from a senior is worth
more than a polished paragraph from a search page. When you teach it,
it is supposed to keep the teaching.

**Bond of minds.** An assigned drive (`GREGW-NORTH`) is one person's
desk copy. The private repository stays with one author so the pack does
not split into five unofficial versions of the truth.

**Aim high, stay humble.** The roadmap is real: a database, a window
instead of a terminal, more manufacturers. None of that is claimed as
shipped. Cheerful is allowed. Fake certainty is not.

**Shop floor.** The teammate path is plug in and talk. The administrator
path is the deploy console. Nobody on a live call should be asked to
become a prompt engineer.

## What we will not do in that name

- Quote or paste the pocketbooks into skills or READMEs.
- Use philosophy language to excuse a missing procedure.
- Treat a forum rumor as a principle.
- Sell the engine as the product.

If you need the books themselves, they live with the company, not in
this Git tree.
